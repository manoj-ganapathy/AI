This skill holds the channel's banked, reusable production decisions so they don't need to be re-derived every time a new video runs through the YouTube Content Engine's 16-state pipeline. Always pair this skill with that pipeline skill — this one supplies the project-specific inputs (States 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 11, 14 in that pipeline are "banked" and should reuse what's here rather than being redone per video).

## Strict script linter checklist (run at State 6 and again before State 16)

Strip bracketed cues before counting (`re.sub(r'\[[^\]]*\]', '', text)`). A failed check means fix it before advancing or exporting. Report pass/fail in the State 6 accessibility review.

1. **Teaser check** — scan every script version (clean, cued, editor's-note) for phrases such as "watch the," "covered recently," "linked in the corner," or any wording that points to another video, playlist, or series. A hit that points elsewhere fails the build. Exempt: the approved generic lines ("I'll see you on the next one"; "tells me exactly what to cover next"). "Watch the" also matches innocent phrases ("watch the caller ID"), so judge each hit in context.
2. **Subscribe count** — exactly one subscribe ask (the closing CTA). More than one fails the build.
3. **Word-count floor** — at least 1,520 words (target band 1,520–1,680). Below the floor fails the build.
4. **Takeaway first** — the first sentence is the protective takeaway, and the takeaway plus preview sentence finish by about 0:15–0:17 (~30–36 words at the deliberate pace; the sample takeaway-plus-preview in the first-15-seconds rule runs 33).
5. **Cold-open pace** — the cold open is 90–100 words, about 45 seconds.
6. **Package** — exactly three Claude Docs for this video (Content Package, Full Script, Editor's-Note Script); no other files; nothing appended to another video's doc.
7. **Placeholders** — every unresolved URL is an exact placeholder token and appears in the [ACTION REQUIRED BEFORE PUBLISHING] callout.

Legacy docs (Videos 1–6) are checked the same way when opened for review, to flag violations (e.g., Video 2's uncaught teaser, the mid-video subscribe nudges in Videos 1–4). Report only; do not edit legacy docs unless the user asks.

## Banked script voice & structure (States 5–6 — reuse, don't redo)

Every script (verified against Video 3 and Video 4 in full) follows the same beat-by-beat shape and reuses several passages verbatim. Write new scripts to this template rather than free-forming the structure each time. As of 2026-09-22 the mid-video subscribe nudge beat is cut and a new "Bridge" beat addressing adult children/caregivers is added after the recap (see the standing production rules above) — the sequence below reflects both changes; Videos 1–4's existing docs still have the old version (mid-video nudge in place, no Bridge beat).

**Beat sequence:**
1. **Cold open** `[Direct to camera, calm but serious energy]` — restructured 2026-09-26 and again 2026-09-28 (see the standing cold-open and first-15-seconds rules above). No logo sting, channel welcome, or generic setup before it. Order: (a) **takeaway first** — the very first sentence is the direct protective takeaway aimed at the viewer's habit ("If you <video-specific habit>, stop doing it today"), delivered as a plain instruction, not a rebuke; (b) **by the 15-second mark**, one preview sentence that names the concrete red-flag detail (what the phone, screen, or document showed, with the specific sourced dollar figure where one exists) and promises the payoff — the mechanic the viewer will see (with a real timestamp if one is named) and that the four named rules are coming (see the front-loading rule); (c) the on-screen **3-point agenda card** appears right after (see Editor's Note types); (d) only then pivot to the viewer with a brief second-person "Imagine..." hypothetical; (e) that flows into a specific, renamed real victim's story (see the no-real-names rule above). No generic warnings and no premise setup ahead of the takeaway. Target ~90–100 words inside a ~45-second window, deliberately slower than ~160 wpm (roughly 30–35 words per 15 seconds). Everything is drawn from this video's own sourced facts and stays entirely on this video's topic (no other-video tease).
2. `[pause]` then the **damage/consequence beat** — what the victim actually lost, a quote from the victim or a family member, how institutions (a bank, a company) responded.
3. `[lean in, slower]` — **"Here's what almost nobody explains..."** mechanism beat: names the scam technique and explains step-by-step how it technically works. **Added 2026-09-28:** also explain the psychological trick that makes it work (e.g., urgency tactics) where the source supports it — see the why-and-how rule.
4. `[beat]` — **first big sourced statistic**, always attributed to a named agency/official/report (never an unsourced number).
5. `[pause]` — a **"sit with that number"** beat that re-emphasizes the scale of the stat just given.
6. `[beat, firmer]` — an **adjacent scam variant or deeper mechanism** beat, with its own named source and stat.
7. `[lean back in, serious]` — **"good news" / enforcement-progress beat**: credit the institutions actively fighting the problem, with sourced progress stats. ## Banked editor's-note script format (a third format, alongside the master cued script and the clean recording-only script)

Built for Video 1 and Video 4 first (user-provided samples), then replicated for Video 2. **Mechanics:**
- Every spoken chunk is tagged `[Spoken]: <text>` on its own line, chunked smaller than the master script's beats — often a single sentence, split wherever a graphic needs to land.

- Build both the chunk boundaries and the note content from the video's **actual full running script** (the Full Script doc), not from any abbreviated "Script Line" column in a shot-list table — those are shortened for reference (literal "..." mid-sentence) and will silently drop words if copied directly. Check programmatically that the chunks, joined, match the running script word for word. (As of 2026-09-22, no new shot-list table is produced at all — this note is retained for any legacy doc that still has one.)
- The "Bridge" beat (beat 11 above) typically gets a **text overlay tag** such as "IF YOU'RE THE ONE WHO SENT THIS — [short takeaway]" — use the Text overlay tag convention below; skip the note if no natural short callout fits that video's specific tip.

- **Title card** — "Display clean minimalist title card fading in: '<short-form title>'" — a short-form version of the locked title (e.g. "The Check Washing Scam", "The New Social Security Phishing Scam"), placed a beat or two after the victim is first named.

## Senior/older-adult accessibility checklist (States 5, 6, 8, 9)

Apply for real on every script — don't rubber-stamp it (no separate shot-list pass exists anymore; verify these directly against the script and its graphic cues):
1. A "grab a pen" pause before the rules section, with a genuine multi-second silence.
2. A full-screen recap card restating the rules near the end.
3. One number per graphic card — never stack multiple stats in a single card.
4. A processing `[pause]` after any big or emotional statistic.
5. Matching graphics for adjacent spoken stats (don't let visuals lag or skip ahead of narration).
6. Non-condescending, non-victim-blaming language throughout — including toward the adult children addressed in the Bridge beat, never framing them as taking control away from a parent.
7. Non-ageist B-roll and imagery.
8. Recommend closed captions on every video.
9. A consistent visual signature across all thumbnails (the raised-hand stop gesture).
10. The Immediate Action Steps checklist (final screen + description) names real, scam-specific agencies/hotlines/URLs — never a generic or copy-pasted list across videos, and never a fabricated citation when a specific one isn't available.
11. Mobile legibility (added 2026-09-28): all on-screen text bold, 60–70pt equivalent or larger, heavy contrast; suspicious lines/amounts zoomed ~200–300%. Check at phone size.
12. Contrast & legibility check (added 2026-09-28; State 6, before export): list the text/background hex pairs used across the video's graphics (e.g., #FFFFFF text on #0A192F, #FF0000 highlight) and verify WCAG contrast: at least 4.5:1 for text (aim for 7:1 or more on key text such as Rule Cards) and at least 3:1 for arrows and highlights against their background. Worked example: #FFFFFF on #0A192F is about 17.6:1 (passes); #FF0000 on #0A192F is about 4.4:1 (fine for an arrow or highlight, but red text on that navy needs a thick outline or backing).

Script word-count target: **~1,600 words ±5% (1,520–1,680)**, mapping to roughly **9:30–10:30 minutes** at the channel's established ~160 words/minute delivery pace (measured from Video 3's and Video 4's actual scripts). 9:30 is an explicit floor, not just the bottom of a target band — write long enough that edit-room trims still land at or above it, since YouTube's real minimum for mid-roll ad eligibility is 8:00. The removed mid-video subscribe nudge beat (~55 words) and the added Bridge beat (~40–60 words) roughly offset each other, so the target range is unchanged. Verified by stripping bracketed cues (`re.sub(r'\[[^\]]*\]', '', text)`) then counting words. **Cold-open pacing (added 2026-09-26):** the cold open is ~90–100 words in ~45 seconds (about 125 wpm, deliberately slower than the ~160 wpm used elsewhere). That costs roughly 10 seconds versus the average pace, but the 1,520–1,680 band still clears the 9:30 floor (e.g., 1,520 words ≈ 9:39 with a 95-word open), so do not trim the total below the low end of the band to compensate. Count the cold open separately when checking the ~90–100 word target.
